The story

The problem

Fantasy apps are good at telling you what already happened. They're bad at telling you what to do next. The stats I wanted were spread across nflverse data, beat reporters, injury reports and coach press conferences, and the projections in the big apps are a black box I couldn't check.

How I thought about it

I wanted one place that answers the weekly questions: who do I pick up, who do I start, who do I trade for. And I wanted every answer to come from data I can see and math I can test, not someone's hot take.

How I got there

It started as NFL_stats, a simple page of tables on GitHub Pages. I build one project at a time and finish it before starting the next: navigation first, then the Lab, the waiver board, the weekly Rundown, then Pressers. Python jobs in GitHub Actions pull fresh data on a schedule so the site keeps itself current. When the scope outgrew football, I renamed it GameDay_stats and moved it to thegameday.zone.

What it does

Waiver board

Ranks pickups for my Sleeper leagues by expected points, usage trends and matchups.

Lab

Query every nflverse stat in the browser: pivots, a DuckDB SQL editor, charts and CSV export.

Pressers

Head coach press conferences pulled daily and summarized with Claude, so injury and role news shows up in one place.

The Rundown

Weekly recap and preview articles for my league, written by Claude from the week's real stats.

Trade and matchups

League and NFL matchups side by side, plus a trade view for comparing players.

Stat guide

A plain-English guide to the roughly 1,500 nflverse stat columns.

Goals

Beat the big apps

A rest-of-season player model that beats ESPN and Sleeper projections, with locked weekly projections as the public track record.

Make the Lab easy

Plain stat names, saved stat sets and more charts, so anyone can ask a question without knowing SQL.

More sports

MLB next season and Premier League fantasy after that, each on its own part of thegameday.zone.

Getting Yahoo API access approved

Sleeper has an open API. Yahoo doesn't. To bring Yahoo leagues into the site, I had to apply to Yahoo for Fantasy Sports API access and get the use case approved.

  1. Applied (Oct 2026)Requested Fantasy Sports API access so friends in Yahoo leagues could use the site with their own leagues.
  2. Approved for personal use (Nov 2026)Yahoo approved the app and I signed their API agreement.
  3. Asked for more (Nov 2026)Emailed Yahoo to ask whether friends could connect their leagues and whether full league rosters were allowed.
  4. Confirmed (Nov 24, 2026)Yahoo confirmed my access under the same agreement.
  5. Built the private Yahoo pageA login-only "My Yahoo team" page running on a Cloudflare Worker behind Cloudflare Access.
