01 · SALES WORK

A sales day that runs off one screen.

My call list lived in HubSpot, the dialer in Nooks, and the context I needed before each call was spread across notes, emails and old activity. So I built boards that pull it into one place: what to do first, who to call, and everything I know about each account.

The problem

Prep ate the morning. Every call meant clicking through records to remember who I talked to, what they said and what I promised. Follow-ups slipped when the day got busy.

What I built

A day board with a morning brief, dials planned by time zone, the call list and full account history side by side. An end of day review that shows dials, conversations and demos booked, and drafts the follow-ups. A prospect page I can send a contractor that shows the product with their own brand on it.

How I got there

I started with the exports I already had, built one view at a time, and kept only what I actually used on calls. Each version came from a real day of dialing.

The goal

Less time prepping, more conversations. Walk into every call already knowing the account.

02 · GAMEDAY_STATS

A fantasy football war room I actually trust.

Fantasy apps tell you what already happened. I wanted something that tells me what to do this week, built on real data, with my own math.

The problem

The data I wanted was split across apps, articles and press conferences, and the projections the big apps give you are a black box.

What I built

A waiver board for my Sleeper leagues, a Lab for querying every nflverse stat with SQL, daily head coach press conference summaries, and weekly recap and preview write-ups. It updates itself with GitHub Actions.

How I got there

It started as NFL_stats, a page of tables. I added one project at a time and finished each before starting the next. I applied for and got Yahoo Fantasy API access, then renamed it GameDay_stats so it can grow into MLB and Premier League fantasy.

The goal

A rest-of-season player model that beats ESPN and Sleeper projections, with locked weekly projections as the track record.

03 · DS COMPANION

A festival app that works when the signal doesn't.

Before Dreamstate SoCal 2026 I wanted to hear every artist on the lineup, plan my sets and share it all with friends. Nothing like that existed, so I made it.

The problem

A lineup full of names I half knew, set times in a flat image, and a venue where phone service dies once the crowd shows up.

What I built

Playlists from my own libraries sorted by set time, 30 second previews, a set time grid where you star your plan, live timing during the show and a festival map. It installs to your home screen like an app.

How I got there

At the festival it loaded blank with weak signal until I switched to airplane mode. That taught me the real lesson: open from the saved copy first, then check the network. I rebuilt it that way.

The goal

Do it again next year, and build companions for other events.

04 · Builds

Small tools, because I didn't want another subscription.

Most of these started the same way: an app wanted a monthly fee for something I could build in an evening.

PhonePad

Turns my iPhone into a trackpad and keyboard for my Mac. It runs locally from a small web app, no account and no app store.

Why: the remote mouse apps I tried all wanted a subscription.

Floating Shorts player

A fork of the open source FullFloatPiP Chrome extension and Mac app. Shorts float over full screen apps with next, previous, skip buttons and global hotkeys.

Why: I didn't want to pay for a picture in picture app to get the controls I wanted.

Chrome extensions

My own versions of paid extensions, starting with the Shorts player. Next up are clean, sellable builds anyone can install.

Why: if I'm building it for myself anyway, it can help other people too.
